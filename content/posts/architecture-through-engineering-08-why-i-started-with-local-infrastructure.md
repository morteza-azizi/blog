# Why I Started With Local Infrastructure

> *Part 8 of the Architecture Through Engineering series.*

## Central Question

Why start locally before moving to the cloud?

## Why This Article Exists

The reference implementation began with local infrastructure instead of Azure. That's a real trade-off, not a default, and it deserves an honest look at what it bought and what it cost.

## The Story

- The case for building against local infrastructure first.
- What became easier because of that choice; what became harder later.
- Where local infrastructure honestly diverges from what production would require.
- Whether this was the right call, argued honestly rather than defended reflexively.

## Key Ideas

- "Local first" is a trade-off with a bill that comes due eventually, not a free win.
- Speed of iteration and fidelity to production pull in opposite directions.
- This decision should be judged by what Part 10 (the Azure migration) reveals, not by this article alone.

## Connection to the Series

Follows the retrieval boundary from Part 7 — local infrastructure is the first concrete implementation behind that boundary. Sets up Part 9, which looks at the abstractions that made this choice possible to defer.

## Open Questions

- Which parts of local infrastructure are closest to production reality, and which are furthest?
- What signal would tell us it's time to move to Azure?
